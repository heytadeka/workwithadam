The New Laziness: AI Scripts, AI Copy, AI Targeting. And Why Your ROAS Is Still Flat.

There used to be a version of lazy that was easy to spot.

Stock photos. Generic hooks. Copy-pasted audience targeting. Ads that looked like they took twenty minutes because they did.

That version of lazy is mostly gone now.

It has been replaced by something that looks like effort. AI-written scripts. AI-generated visuals. Advantage+ campaigns left to optimise themselves. A full creative suite produced in an afternoon.

The output looks busy. The dashboard still looks bad.

The new lazy is harder to spot because it comes with a tool attached.

01 Using a tool is not the same as doing the work

This is the thing that most people miss when they talk about AI in advertising.

The work in advertising has never been production. Production is the easy part. The work is the thinking — understanding who you are talking to, what they care about, why they would stop, and what would make them act.

AI has made production faster. It has not made the thinking easier. It has not done the thinking for you.

So when someone tells me they are using AI to scale their creative — my first question is: what is the brief? What did you give it? What insight is the creative built on?

Most of the time, the answer is something like "I told it to write a hook for a skincare brand."

That is not a brief. That is an instruction to produce average content about a category.

02 AI targeting is not a strategy. It is a default.

Advantage+. Performance Max. Broad match everything. Let the algorithm figure it out.

There are accounts where this works. Mostly accounts with strong creative, strong data, and a product that already converts.

For everyone else, broad AI targeting is just spending more money to find out that your creative does not work.

The algorithm is not magic. It is pattern matching. It finds people who look like people who have already converted — which means if your creative is not converting anyone, you are giving it nothing to work with.

Broad targeting with weak creative is not a growth strategy. It is an expensive way to confirm that something is broken.

The brands that win with automated targeting still know their customer. They still know their angle. They have just earned the right to let the algorithm scale it.

03 AI copy that sounds like AI copy is killing your credibility

People can feel it now.

There is a specific cadence to AI-written ad copy. The way it opens. The rhythm of the sentences. The particular kind of enthusiasm that hits every beat without ever saying anything surprising.

Audiences have been exposed to enough of it that it registers — not consciously, but emotionally. It feels like noise. It feels like nobody is home.

And nothing kills conversion like an ad that feels like it was written for nobody in particular.

The copy that actually works is specific. It uses language the customer recognises. It says something they have not heard before, or says something familiar in a way that surprises them. That specificity does not come from a prompt. It comes from knowing your customer well enough to write like you are talking to one person.

04 Speed is not the constraint. Thinking is.

Most brands were not failing because they could not produce creative fast enough.

They were failing because they did not know what to say.

AI solved a problem that was not the actual problem. Now they can produce the wrong thing faster than ever.

The constraint has always been strategic clarity. What is the one thing this ad needs to do? What does the customer believe right now, and what do we need them to believe? What is the most honest, compelling version of this offer?

Answer those questions and the creative almost writes itself. Skip them and no tool will save you.

05 What hard work actually looks like now

I am not saying do not use AI. I use it. It is genuinely useful.

But the brands separating themselves right now are the ones putting the hard work where it matters — before the tools come out.

They are:

  • reading their own reviews obsessively
  • talking to customers and actually listening
  • understanding what objections kill the sale
  • building a creative POV, not just a creative calendar
  • testing angles, not just formats

That is the work. It is not glamorous and it is not fast. But it is what makes the AI output actually good — because you are feeding it real insight instead of asking it to guess.

AI is only as good as the thinking you bring to it. Most people bring none.

Final thought

The new lazy looks productive. That is what makes it dangerous.

You can run a full creative operation — scripts, copy, targeting, optimisation — entirely on autopilot now. It will feel like you are doing the work. The ROAS will tell you otherwise.

The shortcut was always the problem. AI has just made the shortcut look more impressive.

Do the thinking first. Use the tools second. That order is not optional.
